# Falls in hospital: after a fall

Source: based on NICE guidance and quality standard QS86 on falls in older people and the Royal College of Physicians National Audit of Inpatient Falls. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local post-falls protocol.

## Immediately after a fall
Do not move the patient until they have been checked for injury. Assess with ABCDE and check for signs of fracture, including hip fracture (pain, a shortened and externally rotated leg, inability to bear weight), and for signs of spinal injury. If a hip or spinal fracture is suspected, use safe flat-lifting equipment to move the patient, not a hoist that lifts them from sitting.

## Head injury after a fall
Check for signs of head injury, especially if the fall was unwitnessed. Start neurological observations and get a medical review if a head injury is suspected or cannot be ruled out, particularly in patients taking anticoagulant or antiplatelet medicines. Follow NICE head injury guidance for CT scanning.

## Medical review
The patient should have a medical review after the fall, looking at injuries, the cause of the fall (for example postural hypotension, infection, delirium, arrhythmia or medicines), and the plan. Record observations, including lying and standing blood pressure when it is safe.

## Preventing further falls
Review falls risk factors: medicines that increase falls risk, continence and toileting, footwear, vision, delirium, mobility aids, bed height and the call bell within reach. Tell the patient's family or carers, complete an incident report, and update the care plan.
