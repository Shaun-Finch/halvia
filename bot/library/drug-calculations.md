# Drug and infusion calculations

Source: standard formulae used in UK nursing and medical education. Summary written by Halvia. Always double-check calculations and follow your local policy for independent second checks.

## Dose from weight
Dose = dose per kg x weight in kg. For example, 0.1 units/kg/hour for a 70 kg adult is 7 units per hour.

## Volume to give
Volume to give = (dose needed / stock strength) x stock volume. For example, if you need 500 micrograms and the stock is 1 mg (1000 micrograms) in 1 mL, give (500 / 1000) x 1 mL = 0.5 mL.

## Infusion pump rate
Rate in mL per hour = volume in mL / time in hours. For example, 1000 mL over 8 hours = 125 mL per hour. For a drug infusion: rate in mL/hour = dose needed per hour / concentration per mL.

## Gravity drip rate
Drops per minute = (volume in mL x drop factor) / time in minutes. Standard giving sets are usually 20 drops/mL for clear fluids and 15 drops/mL for blood. For example, 1000 mL over 8 hours with a 20 drop/mL set = (1000 x 20) / 480 = about 42 drops per minute.

## Unit conversions
1 gram = 1000 milligrams. 1 milligram = 1000 micrograms. 1 litre = 1000 mL. Write "micrograms" and "units" in full; do not abbreviate them, to avoid errors. A percentage solution is grams per 100 mL: 1% = 10 mg/mL, so 10% calcium gluconate contains 100 mg/mL. A ratio of 1 in 1000 means 1 g in 1000 mL, which is 1 mg/mL.
