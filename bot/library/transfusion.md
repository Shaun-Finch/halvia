# Blood transfusion and major haemorrhage

Source: based on NICE guideline NG24, Blood transfusion (2015), and the CRASH-2 trial on tranexamic acid in trauma. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your hospital transfusion policy and major haemorrhage protocol.

## Red cell thresholds
For adults who need a red cell transfusion and do not have major haemorrhage, acute coronary syndrome or chronic transfusion needs, use a restrictive threshold of 70 g/L and a target of 70 to 90 g/L after transfusion. For people with acute coronary syndrome, consider a threshold of 80 g/L and a target of 80 to 100 g/L. Give single units to non-bleeding adults and reassess before giving more.

## Safe transfusion
Use positive patient identification at every step, checking identity against the wristband and the blood component at the bedside. Record observations before the transfusion, at 15 minutes after the start of each unit, and at the end, and observe the patient throughout. If a reaction is suspected (fever, rigors, breathlessness, hypotension, rash, pain), stop the transfusion, assess with ABCDE, check the patient and unit details, and get urgent medical review.

## Tranexamic acid
Offer tranexamic acid to adults having surgery who are expected to have at least moderate blood loss (more than 500 mL). In major trauma, give tranexamic acid as soon as possible and within 3 hours of injury: 1 g IV over 10 minutes, then 1 g over 8 hours.

## Major haemorrhage
Activate the major haemorrhage protocol early. Control the bleeding source, keep the patient warm, give blood components according to the protocol, and monitor calcium, coagulation and haemoglobin. Involve senior and specialist teams.
