# Hyponatraemia in adults

Source: based on the European Society of Endocrinology, European Society of Intensive Care Medicine and European Renal Association clinical practice guideline on the diagnosis and treatment of hyponatraemia (2014). Summary written by Halvia. Seek senior and specialist advice.

## Definitions
Hyponatraemia is a serum sodium below 135 mmol/L. Mild is 130 to 135 mmol/L, moderate is 125 to 129 mmol/L, and profound is below 125 mmol/L. Acute hyponatraemia has developed within 48 hours; otherwise it is chronic.

## Severe symptoms: an emergency
Severe symptoms include vomiting, cardiorespiratory distress, abnormal and deep somnolence, seizures and a reduced GCS (9 or less). Give 150 mL of 3% hypertonic saline IV over 20 minutes, check sodium, and repeat if needed, aiming for a rise of 5 mmol/L in the first hour. Manage in a high-dependency setting.

## Safe rate of correction
Limit the rise in serum sodium to 10 mmol/L in the first 24 hours and 8 mmol/L in every 24 hours after that, because overly fast correction can cause osmotic demyelination syndrome. People at higher risk of this include those with alcohol misuse, malnutrition, hypokalaemia and liver disease.

## Finding the cause
Assess fluid status and check serum osmolality, urine osmolality and urine sodium. Common causes include diuretics (especially thiazides), SIADH (for example from drugs, pneumonia or brain injury), hypovolaemia, heart failure, cirrhosis, adrenal insufficiency and hypothyroidism. Review all medicines.
