# Convulsive status epilepticus in adults

Source: based on NICE guideline NG217, Epilepsies in children, young people and adults (2022), and the ESETT trial. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local status epilepticus protocol and check doses in the BNF.

## Definition
Convulsive status epilepticus is a generalised tonic-clonic seizure lasting 5 minutes or longer, or repeated seizures without recovery in between. Start treatment at 5 minutes.

## Immediate care
Note the time the seizure started. Protect from injury, maintain the airway, give high-flow oxygen, and check capillary blood glucose. Treat hypoglycaemia with IV glucose. If alcohol misuse or malnutrition is possible, give IV thiamine (such as Pabrinex) before or with glucose. Get IV access and send bloods, including electrolytes, calcium, magnesium and anti-seizure drug levels where relevant.

## First line: benzodiazepine
If there is IV access, give IV lorazepam 0.1 mg/kg (usually 4 mg in an adult). If there is no IV access, give buccal midazolam (10 mg in an adult) or rectal diazepam. If the seizure continues 5 to 10 minutes after the first dose, give one further dose. Give no more than two doses of benzodiazepine, including any given before arrival.

## Second line
If seizures continue after two doses of benzodiazepine, give one of levetiracetam, phenytoin or sodium valproate IV. Doses used in trials and many UK protocols are levetiracetam 60 mg/kg (maximum 4,500 mg), phenytoin 20 mg/kg with ECG monitoring, or sodium valproate 40 mg/kg (maximum 3,000 mg). Avoid valproate in women and girls who could become pregnant unless there is no alternative. If the second-line drug fails, consider a different second-line drug.

## Third line
If status epilepticus continues, contact critical care: general anaesthesia (such as propofol, midazolam or thiopental) is needed for refractory status epilepticus, usually within 30 to 60 minutes of onset. Look for and treat the cause.
