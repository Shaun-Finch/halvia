# Acute kidney injury (AKI)

Source: based on NICE guideline NG148, Acute kidney injury: prevention, detection and management (2019), and the KDIGO definition. Summary written by Halvia. Check the current NICE guideline and your local AKI pathway.

## Definition
AKI is detected by any of the following: a rise in serum creatinine of 26 micromol/L or more within 48 hours; a 50% or greater rise in serum creatinine known or presumed to have occurred within the past 7 days; or a fall in urine output to less than 0.5 mL/kg per hour for more than 6 hours in adults. Many labs issue automated AKI warning stages 1 to 3.

## Who is at risk
Risk factors include chronic kidney disease, heart failure, liver disease, diabetes, age 65 or over, a history of AKI, sepsis, hypovolaemia, cognitive impairment that limits access to fluids, use of nephrotoxic drugs, iodinated contrast, and urological obstruction.

## Initial management
Assess fluid status and treat hypovolaemia. Look for and treat sepsis. Review medicines: consider stopping or holding nephrotoxic drugs and drugs that can make AKI worse, such as NSAIDs, ACE inhibitors, angiotensin receptor blockers and diuretics, and review drugs that accumulate, such as metformin. Dipstick the urine. Monitor urine output, fluid balance, creatinine and potassium.

## Imaging and referral
Offer urinary tract ultrasound within 24 hours if the cause is not known or obstruction is suspected, and immediately if pyonephrosis is suspected. Refer urgently to the renal team for hyperkalaemia, metabolic acidosis, fluid overload or uraemic complications that do not respond to treatment, or if the cause is unclear or intrinsic renal disease is suspected.
