# Adult cardiac arrest: advanced life support

Source: based on Resuscitation Council UK, Adult advanced life support guidelines (2021). Summary written by Halvia. Follow current Resuscitation Council UK guidance and your local training.

## Recognition and first steps
If a patient is unresponsive and not breathing normally, shout for help, call the resuscitation team (2222 in most UK hospitals) and start CPR at 30 compressions to 2 breaths. Attach a defibrillator or monitor as soon as possible. Minimise interruptions to chest compressions.

## High-quality CPR
Compress the centre of the chest to a depth of 5 to 6 cm at a rate of 100 to 120 compressions per minute, allowing full recoil. Change the person doing compressions every 2 minutes. Once an advanced airway is in place, give continuous compressions and ventilate at about 10 breaths per minute. Use waveform capnography.

## Shockable rhythms: VF or pulseless VT
Give one shock, then immediately resume CPR for 2 minutes without pausing to check the rhythm. Pause briefly to assess the rhythm every 2 minutes. Give adrenaline 1 mg IV or IO after the third shock, then every 3 to 5 minutes. Give amiodarone 300 mg IV after three shocks, and a further 150 mg after five shocks.

## Non-shockable rhythms: PEA or asystole
Give adrenaline 1 mg IV or IO as soon as access is obtained, then every 3 to 5 minutes. Continue CPR, checking the rhythm every 2 minutes.

## Reversible causes: the 4 Hs and 4 Ts
Look for and treat reversible causes. The 4 Hs are hypoxia, hypovolaemia, hypo- or hyperkalaemia and other metabolic disorders, and hypothermia or hyperthermia. The 4 Ts are thrombosis (coronary or pulmonary), tension pneumothorax, tamponade (cardiac) and toxins. Consider point-of-care ultrasound in skilled hands.

## After return of spontaneous circulation
Use the ABCDE approach, aim for oxygen saturation of 94 to 98%, normal PaCO2, a 12-lead ECG, and treat the cause. Aim for a systolic blood pressure above 100 mmHg. Refer for critical care and consider coronary angiography.
