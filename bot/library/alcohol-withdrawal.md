# Alcohol withdrawal and Wernicke's encephalopathy

Source: based on NICE clinical guideline CG100, Alcohol-use disorders: diagnosis and management of physical complications (updated 2017). Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local alcohol withdrawal protocol.

## Timing and features
Withdrawal symptoms usually start 6 to 12 hours after the last drink: tremor, sweating, anxiety, nausea, tachycardia and hypertension. Withdrawal seizures can occur, typically within 48 hours. Delirium tremens (confusion, hallucinations, severe agitation and autonomic instability) usually appears at 48 to 72 hours and is a medical emergency.

## Assessment
Ask about the amount drunk, the time of the last drink and previous withdrawal, seizures or delirium tremens. Use a validated tool such as CIWA-Ar to measure severity and guide treatment.

## Treatment
Offer a benzodiazepine (often chlordiazepoxide) or clomethiazole for acute withdrawal, using a symptom-triggered regimen or a fixed-dose reducing regimen according to local protocol. Treat delirium tremens with oral lorazepam, and parenteral lorazepam or haloperidol if symptoms persist or oral medicine is declined. Treat withdrawal seizures with a quick-acting benzodiazepine such as lorazepam.

## Thiamine and Wernicke's encephalopathy
Offer oral thiamine to people at risk who are harmful or dependent drinkers and malnourished or at risk, or have decompensated liver disease, or are in acute withdrawal. Wernicke's encephalopathy can present with any of confusion, ataxia, ophthalmoplegia or nystagmus, but most patients do not have all three. If it is suspected, give parenteral thiamine (such as Pabrinex) promptly, and give it before any glucose in at-risk patients.
