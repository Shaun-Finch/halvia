# NEWS2: National Early Warning Score 2

Source: Royal College of Physicians. National Early Warning Score (NEWS) 2: Standardising the assessment of acute-illness severity in the NHS. Updated report of a working party. London: RCP, 2017. Summary written by Halvia. Always check the current RCP chart and your local escalation policy.

## What NEWS2 is
NEWS2 is a track-and-trigger score for adults (aged 16 and over) that turns seven routine observations into one number. A higher score means a higher risk of deterioration and a more urgent clinical response. It is not validated for children or for use in pregnancy, where paediatric and obstetric early warning scores are used instead.

## The seven parameters
NEWS2 scores respiration rate, oxygen saturation, whether the patient is on air or supplemental oxygen, systolic blood pressure, pulse rate, level of consciousness or new confusion, and temperature. Each parameter scores 0 to 3 and the scores are added together.

## Respiration rate (breaths per minute)
8 or less scores 3. 9 to 11 scores 1. 12 to 20 scores 0. 21 to 24 scores 2. 25 or more scores 3.

## Oxygen saturation: SpO2 Scale 1
Scale 1 is used for most patients. 91% or less scores 3. 92 to 93% scores 2. 94 to 95% scores 1. 96% or more scores 0.

## Oxygen saturation: SpO2 Scale 2
Scale 2 is only for patients with confirmed hypercapnic respiratory failure who have a prescribed target saturation of 88 to 92%, decided by a competent clinical decision maker and recorded in the notes. 83% or less scores 3. 84 to 85% scores 2. 86 to 87% scores 1. 88 to 92%, or 93% or more on air, scores 0. On oxygen: 93 to 94% scores 1, 95 to 96% scores 2, and 97% or more scores 3.

## Air or oxygen
Any supplemental oxygen scores 2. Breathing air scores 0.

## Systolic blood pressure (mmHg)
90 or less scores 3. 91 to 100 scores 2. 101 to 110 scores 1. 111 to 219 scores 0. 220 or more scores 3.

## Pulse (beats per minute)
40 or less scores 3. 41 to 50 scores 1. 51 to 90 scores 0. 91 to 110 scores 1. 111 to 130 scores 2. 131 or more scores 3.

## Consciousness
Alert scores 0. New confusion, or responding only to voice or pain, or unresponsive (the C, V, P or U of ACVPU) scores 3. New confusion counts even if the patient appears alert.

## Temperature (degrees Celsius)
35.0 or less scores 3. 35.1 to 36.0 scores 1. 36.1 to 38.0 scores 0. 38.1 to 39.0 scores 1. 39.1 or more scores 2.

## Clinical risk and response thresholds
An aggregate score of 0 to 4 is low risk and needs a ward-based response, with assessment by a registered nurse. A score of 3 in any single parameter is low-medium risk and needs an urgent ward-based response, with review by a clinician who decides whether to escalate. An aggregate score of 5 or 6 is medium risk and is a key threshold for an urgent response: urgent review by a clinician competent in assessing acutely ill patients. An aggregate score of 7 or more is high risk and needs an emergency response: emergency assessment by a team with critical care competencies, usually with transfer to a higher level of care considered.

## Minimum frequency of observations
Score 0: at least every 12 hours. Score 1 to 4: at least every 4 to 6 hours. A score of 3 in a single parameter: at least every hour. Score 5 or 6: at least every hour. Score 7 or more: continuous monitoring of vital signs.

## Sepsis and NEWS2
The RCP advises that a NEWS2 of 5 or more in a patient with suspected infection should prompt consideration of sepsis. A rising score matters as well as the absolute number, and clinical concern should trigger escalation whatever the score.
