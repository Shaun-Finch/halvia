# Stroke and TIA: recognition and early management

Source: based on NICE guideline NG128, Stroke and transient ischaemic attack in over 16s: diagnosis and initial management (2019, updated 2022), and NICE technology appraisal TA990 on tenecteplase (2024). Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local stroke pathway and the current NICE guidance.

## Recognition
Use a validated tool such as FAST (Face, Arm, Speech, Time to call 999) outside hospital and ROSIER in the emergency department. Check blood glucose to exclude hypoglycaemia as a cause of the symptoms. Record the time of onset or the time the person was last known to be well.

## Brain imaging
Perform non-enhanced CT immediately (ideally the next slot and within 1 hour of arrival) if any of these apply: the person could be eligible for thrombolysis or thrombectomy; they are on anticoagulants or have a known bleeding tendency; their GCS is below 13; they have unexplained progressive or fluctuating symptoms; papilloedema, neck stiffness or fever; or severe headache at onset. CT angiography is used when thrombectomy might be indicated.

## Thrombolysis and thrombectomy
Thrombolysis with alteplase or tenecteplase can be given for acute ischaemic stroke when treatment starts within 4.5 hours of known onset and intracranial haemorrhage has been excluded, in a centre with the right expertise. Thrombectomy is offered for proximal anterior circulation occlusion as soon as possible and within 6 hours of onset, and up to 24 hours in selected people where imaging shows salvageable brain tissue.

## Aspirin and early care
Once haemorrhage has been excluded, give aspirin 300 mg orally or rectally as soon as possible and within 24 hours (if thrombolysed, start 24 hours after thrombolysis once a follow-up scan excludes bleeding), continued for 2 weeks. Screen swallowing before giving food, drink or oral medicines. Admit directly to a specialist acute stroke unit.

## Transient ischaemic attack (TIA)
Offer aspirin 300 mg immediately unless contraindicated, and refer for specialist assessment within 24 hours of symptom onset. Do not use scores such as ABCD2 to assess risk. Advise people not to drive until seen by a specialist.
