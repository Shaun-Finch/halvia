# SBAR: structured handover and escalation

Source: SBAR was developed in the US Navy and adapted for healthcare by Kaiser Permanente; it is widely used across the NHS and recommended by NHS England and the Institute for Healthcare Improvement. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your organisation's handover policy.

## What SBAR is
SBAR stands for Situation, Background, Assessment and Recommendation. It is a structured way to pass on information about a patient, especially when escalating a concern by phone or at handover, so the receiver gets the key facts in a predictable order.

## Situation
Say who you are, where you are calling from and which patient you are calling about. State the problem in one sentence. For example: "I'm the staff nurse on Ward 7. I'm calling about Mrs A in bed 4. Her NEWS2 has risen from 2 to 6 in the last four hours and I'm worried she is deteriorating."

## Background
Give the relevant context: reason for admission and date, relevant past history, current treatment and medications, allergies, resuscitation status, and recent changes such as new results or procedures.

## Assessment
Give the current observations and score, including the trend. Describe what you have found on assessment using ABCDE, and what you think is happening: "I think she may be septic" or "I'm not sure what is wrong but I am concerned."

## Recommendation
Say clearly what you need and by when: "Please come and review her within 30 minutes." Ask what to do in the meantime, agree when observations will be repeated and what would trigger a further call.

## Closing the loop
Read back the plan to confirm you have understood it, record the call in the notes with the time and the name of the person you spoke to, and escalate further if the review does not happen in the agreed time.

## Tips
Have the notes, observation chart, drug chart and recent results to hand before calling. Lead with the score and the trend. If you are worried, say so plainly: concern is a valid reason to escalate.
