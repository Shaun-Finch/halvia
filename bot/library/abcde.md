# ABCDE assessment of the acutely unwell adult

Source: based on the ABCDE approach taught by the Resuscitation Council UK. Summary written by Halvia. Follow current Resuscitation Council UK guidance and your local training.

## Principles
The ABCDE approach is a systematic way to assess and treat an acutely ill patient. Assess Airway, Breathing, Circulation, Disability and Exposure in order, treat problems as you find them before moving on, reassess regularly, and call for help early. Check the scene is safe and wear appropriate personal protective equipment. If the patient is unresponsive and not breathing normally, start CPR and call the resuscitation team.

## Airway
Look for signs of airway obstruction: noisy breathing such as stridor, gurgling or snoring, see-saw chest movement, or a patient who cannot speak. Treat with simple manoeuvres such as head tilt and chin lift or jaw thrust, suction, and airway adjuncts, and get expert help for a threatened airway. Give high-concentration oxygen to critically ill patients, then titrate to target saturations.

## Breathing
Count the respiratory rate, check oxygen saturation and the oxygen being given, look at chest movement and work of breathing, and listen to the chest. Look for cyanosis, use of accessory muscles, tracheal position and signs of pneumothorax, bronchospasm or fluid. Consider arterial blood gases and a chest X-ray.

## Circulation
Check the colour and temperature of the hands, capillary refill time (normally 2 seconds or less), pulse rate and rhythm, and blood pressure. Look for signs of bleeding and check urine output. Insert a cannula, take bloods, and give fluids if the patient is hypovolaemic, reassessing after each bolus. Consider an ECG.

## Disability
Assess the level of consciousness using ACVPU or the Glasgow Coma Scale, check pupils, and check the blood glucose: treat hypoglycaemia promptly. Review medicines that might cause reduced consciousness, such as opioids or sedatives.

## Exposure
Examine the patient fully while respecting dignity and avoiding heat loss. Look for rashes, bleeding, swelling, wounds, drains and signs of infection, and measure the temperature.

## After the assessment
Review the notes, charts and results, communicate clearly using SBAR, document what you found and did, and agree a plan for the level of care and how often observations will be repeated.
