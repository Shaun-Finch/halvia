# Infection prevention and control on the ward

Source: based on the WHO 5 Moments for Hand Hygiene and the NHS England National Infection Prevention and Control Manual. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local infection prevention policy.

## The 5 moments for hand hygiene
Clean your hands: 1, before touching a patient; 2, before a clean or aseptic procedure; 3, after body fluid exposure risk; 4, after touching a patient; and 5, after touching a patient's surroundings. Use alcohol hand rub for most moments, and soap and water when hands are visibly dirty, and when caring for patients with diarrhoea or suspected Clostridioides difficile or norovirus, because alcohol does not kill spores.

## Standard precautions
Use personal protective equipment according to the risk: gloves and apron for contact with blood and body fluids, and eye protection where there is a risk of splashing. Dispose of sharps immediately at the point of use. Manage spills, linen and waste safely.

## Isolation
Consider single-room isolation for patients with suspected or known infections that spread easily, such as diarrhoea and vomiting, C. difficile, MRSA in some settings, influenza, COVID-19, tuberculosis and carbapenemase-producing organisms. Follow transmission-based precautions (contact, droplet or airborne) and ask the infection prevention team for advice.

## Sharps injury
After a needlestick or sharps injury, encourage bleeding, wash with soap and running water, do not scrub or suck the wound, report it immediately, and contact occupational health or the emergency department urgently, as post-exposure prophylaxis for HIV works best when started within hours.
