# Acute asthma in adults

Source: based on the BTS/SIGN British guideline on the management of asthma (SIGN 158) and the 2024 BTS/NICE/SIGN asthma guideline. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local acute asthma protocol.

## Assessing severity
Moderate acute asthma: increasing symptoms, peak expiratory flow (PEF) above 50 to 75% of best or predicted, and no features of acute severe asthma. Acute severe asthma: any one of PEF 33 to 50% of best or predicted, respiratory rate 25 per minute or more, heart rate 110 per minute or more, or inability to complete sentences in one breath. Life-threatening asthma: any one of PEF below 33%, SpO2 below 92%, PaO2 below 8 kPa, a normal PaCO2 (4.6 to 6.0 kPa), silent chest, cyanosis, poor respiratory effort, arrhythmia, exhaustion, altered conscious level or hypotension. Near-fatal asthma: raised PaCO2 or the need for mechanical ventilation.

## Treatment
Give oxygen to keep SpO2 at 94 to 98%. Give high-dose inhaled short-acting beta-2 agonist: salbutamol 5 mg nebulised, driven by oxygen, repeated every 15 to 30 minutes or continuously if needed. Add nebulised ipratropium bromide 0.5 mg 4 to 6-hourly for acute severe or life-threatening asthma, or poor response to beta-2 agonist. Give steroids: prednisolone 40 to 50 mg daily for at least 5 days, or IV hydrocortisone 100 mg 6-hourly if unable to take tablets. Consider a single dose of IV magnesium sulfate 1.2 to 2 g over 20 minutes for acute severe or life-threatening asthma that has not responded to initial treatment, after senior advice.

## Escalation
Involve senior staff and critical care early for life-threatening or near-fatal features, deteriorating PEF, worsening hypoxia, hypercapnia, exhaustion, drowsiness or confusion. Get an arterial blood gas if SpO2 is below 92% or there are life-threatening features.

## Discharge
Before discharge, check inhaler technique, give a personal asthma action plan, make sure the patient has steroid tablets and arrange follow-up with their GP or asthma nurse within 2 working days.
