# Head injury: assessment and CT in adults

Source: based on NICE guideline NG232, Head injury: assessment and early management (2023). Summary written by Halvia. Check the current NICE guideline and your local pathway.

## CT head within 1 hour
For adults with a head injury, perform a CT head scan within 1 hour of a risk factor being identified if any of these are present: GCS below 13 on initial assessment in the emergency department; GCS below 15 at 2 hours after the injury; suspected open or depressed skull fracture; any sign of basal skull fracture (haemotympanum, panda eyes, cerebrospinal fluid leaking from the ear or nose, Battle's sign); post-traumatic seizure; focal neurological deficit; or more than one episode of vomiting.

## CT head within 8 hours
For adults with loss of consciousness or amnesia since the injury and any of the following, perform CT within 8 hours of the injury: age 65 or over; a history of bleeding or clotting disorders; a dangerous mechanism of injury; or more than 30 minutes of retrograde amnesia. NICE also advises considering CT within 8 hours for people taking anticoagulant medicines, even without other risk factors. Check the current guideline for the detail.

## Neurological observations
For patients with a GCS of 15, do observations half-hourly for 2 hours, then hourly for 4 hours, then 2-hourly. Observations include GCS, pupil size and reactivity, limb movements, respiratory rate, heart rate, blood pressure, temperature and oxygen saturation. If GCS falls below 15 after the initial 2-hour period, go back to half-hourly observations.

## When to escalate
Ask for urgent review if there is agitation or abnormal behaviour, a sustained drop of 1 point in GCS (especially motor), any drop of 3 or more points in the eye or verbal response or 2 or more in the motor response, severe or increasing headache or persistent vomiting, or new neurological symptoms such as unequal pupils or weakness.
