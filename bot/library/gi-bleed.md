# Acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding

Source: based on NICE clinical guideline CG141, Acute upper gastrointestinal bleeding in over 16s: management (updated 2016), and NICE NG24 on blood transfusion. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local GI bleed pathway.

## Risk assessment
Use the Glasgow-Blatchford score at first assessment and the full Rockall score after endoscopy. Consider early discharge for people with a pre-endoscopy Blatchford score of 0.

## Resuscitation
Assess with ABCDE, insert two large-bore cannulae, and send bloods including full blood count, urea and electrolytes, liver function, clotting and group and save or crossmatch. Use a major haemorrhage protocol if bleeding is massive. Transfuse red cells based on the clinical picture: for patients without major haemorrhage, a restrictive threshold of 70 g/L is usual (80 g/L in acute coronary syndrome).

## Endoscopy
Offer endoscopy immediately after resuscitation to unstable patients with severe acute upper GI bleeding, and within 24 hours of admission to all other patients with upper GI bleeding.

## Variceal bleeding
In suspected or confirmed variceal bleeding (for example with known liver disease), offer terlipressin and prophylactic antibiotics at presentation. Use band ligation for oesophageal varices. Do not offer proton pump inhibitors before endoscopy to patients with suspected non-variceal bleeding.
