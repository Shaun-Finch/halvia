# Oxygen therapy in adults

Source: based on the British Thoracic Society guideline for oxygen use in healthcare and emergency settings (2017). Summary written by Halvia. Oxygen should be prescribed with a target saturation range.

## Target saturations
For most acutely ill adults, aim for an oxygen saturation of 94 to 98%. For people at risk of hypercapnic (type 2) respiratory failure, aim for 88 to 92% until blood gases are known. People at risk include those with COPD, severe chest wall or spinal disease, neuromuscular disease, severe obesity, cystic fibrosis and bronchiectasis.

## Critical illness
In critical illness, such as cardiac arrest, shock, major trauma, sepsis, anaphylaxis or near-drowning, start with high-concentration oxygen from a reservoir mask at 15 litres per minute, then adjust to the target range once the patient is stable.

## Devices
Nasal cannulae deliver about 24 to 40% oxygen at 1 to 6 litres per minute. A simple face mask at 5 to 10 litres per minute gives about 40 to 60%. Venturi masks give a fixed concentration of 24, 28, 35, 40 or 60%. A reservoir mask at 15 litres per minute gives about 60 to 90%.

## Monitoring
Monitor oxygen saturation and respiratory rate regularly, and record oxygen on the NEWS2 chart. A patient who needs increasing oxygen to stay in their target range is deteriorating and needs urgent clinical review. Do not give oxygen to patients who are not hypoxaemic just because they are breathless.
