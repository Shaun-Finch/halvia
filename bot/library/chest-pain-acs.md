# Acute chest pain and acute coronary syndrome

Source: based on NICE guidelines CG95 (Recent-onset chest pain of suspected cardiac origin), NG185 (Acute coronary syndromes, 2020) and the European Society of Cardiology. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local chest pain and primary PCI pathway.

## Immediate assessment
Assess with ABCDE. Record a 12-lead ECG as soon as possible and within 10 minutes of arrival, and repeat it if pain recurs or the first ECG is not diagnostic. Take blood for high-sensitivity troponin using your local rapid rule-out pathway. A normal ECG does not exclude acute coronary syndrome.

## Immediate treatment
Give a single loading dose of aspirin 300 mg as soon as possible unless the person is allergic. Relieve pain with glyceryl trinitrate (GTN) and, if needed, IV opioids. Do not routinely give oxygen: give it only if oxygen saturation is below 94%, aiming for 94 to 98%, or 88 to 92% in people at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure.

## ST-elevation myocardial infarction (STEMI)
Offer coronary angiography with primary percutaneous coronary intervention (PCI) if the person presents within 12 hours of symptom onset and PCI can be delivered within 120 minutes of the time when fibrinolysis could have been given. Otherwise offer fibrinolysis. Contact the heart attack centre early.

## Unstable angina and NSTEMI
Assess the risk of future adverse cardiovascular events using a scoring system such as GRACE. Give antithrombotic therapy according to local protocol and consider angiography within 72 hours for people at intermediate or higher risk.

## Other causes not to miss
Consider aortic dissection (tearing pain, pulse or blood pressure differences between arms), pulmonary embolism, tension pneumothorax, pericarditis with tamponade and oesophageal rupture.
