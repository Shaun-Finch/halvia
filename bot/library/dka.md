# Diabetic ketoacidosis (DKA) in adults

Source: based on the Joint British Diabetes Societies for Inpatient Care (JBDS-IP), The management of diabetic ketoacidosis in adults. Summary written by Halvia. Always follow your local DKA protocol and the current JBDS guideline.

## Diagnosis
DKA is diagnosed when all three are present: ketonaemia of 3.0 mmol/L or more, or significant ketonuria (2+ or more on a urine dipstick); blood glucose above 11.0 mmol/L or known diabetes; and bicarbonate below 15.0 mmol/L and/or venous pH below 7.3. DKA can occur with a normal glucose, for example with SGLT2 inhibitors or in pregnancy.

## Severe DKA
Signs of severe DKA that need senior review and consideration of a higher level of care include blood ketones above 6.0 mmol/L, bicarbonate below 5.0 mmol/L, venous pH below 7.0, potassium below 3.5 mmol/L on admission, GCS below 12, oxygen saturation below 92% on air, systolic blood pressure below 90 mmHg, pulse above 100 or below 60, and an anion gap above 16.

## Fluids
Restore circulating volume with 0.9% sodium chloride. If systolic blood pressure is below 90 mmHg, give 500 mL over 10 to 15 minutes and reassess. A typical regimen for an adult then gives 1 litre over the first hour, then 1 litre over the next 2 hours, 1 litre over 2 hours, 1 litre over 4 hours, 1 litre over 4 hours and 1 litre over 6 hours. Use more caution in older adults, young adults, pregnancy and heart or kidney failure.

## Insulin
Start a fixed-rate intravenous insulin infusion (FRIII) at 0.1 units per kg per hour, using the patient's estimated weight. Continue the patient's usual long-acting basal insulin. When blood glucose falls below 14.0 mmol/L, add 10% glucose at 125 mL per hour alongside the sodium chloride.

## Potassium
Potassium falls as insulin works. If potassium is above 5.5 mmol/L, give no potassium in the fluids. If potassium is 3.5 to 5.5 mmol/L, add 40 mmol/L of potassium chloride to the fluids. If potassium is below 3.5 mmol/L, get senior review, as extra potassium is needed.

## Monitoring and resolution
Check capillary ketones and glucose hourly, and venous gases for pH, bicarbonate and potassium at 60 minutes, 2 hours and then 2-hourly. Targets are a fall in blood ketones of at least 0.5 mmol/L per hour, a rise in bicarbonate of 3.0 mmol/L per hour, and a fall in glucose of 3.0 mmol/L per hour. DKA has resolved when ketones are below 0.6 mmol/L and venous pH is above 7.3. Involve the diabetes team.
