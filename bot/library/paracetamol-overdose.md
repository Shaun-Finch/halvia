# Paracetamol overdose

Source: based on MHRA advice on intravenous acetylcysteine for paracetamol overdose (2012) and the UK National Poisons Information Service (TOXBASE). Summary written by Halvia. Always check TOXBASE and your local protocol: treatment details, including the acetylcysteine regimen, vary between hospitals.

## Key principles
Note the time of ingestion, the total amount taken in mg/kg, and whether it was a single acute ingestion or staggered over more than 1 hour. Use the patient's weight, capped according to TOXBASE for heavier patients.

## Timed single overdose
Measure plasma paracetamol at 4 hours or later after ingestion; levels taken before 4 hours cannot be interpreted. Plot the level on the treatment nomogram: give acetylcysteine if the level is on or above the single treatment line, which starts at 100 mg/L at 4 hours. Do not wait for the level if the patient presents 8 hours or more after ingestion of a potentially toxic amount: start acetylcysteine straight away and review when results are back.

## Staggered or uncertain timing
If the overdose was staggered or the timing is uncertain, the nomogram cannot be used. Treat with acetylcysteine without delay where a potentially toxic dose may have been taken, and check TOXBASE.

## Acetylcysteine
Acetylcysteine is given intravenously, using either the traditional three-bag 21-hour regimen or a two-bag 12-hour regimen (SNAP), according to local protocol. Reactions such as flushing, rash, wheeze or hypotension are usually not true allergy: pause the infusion and treat, then restart at a slower rate in most cases.

## Blood tests and follow-up
Check paracetamol level, liver function, INR, renal function, glucose and venous gas. Before stopping treatment, check that bloods are within acceptable limits per TOXBASE. Assess mental health and risk before discharge, and involve the liaison psychiatry team.
