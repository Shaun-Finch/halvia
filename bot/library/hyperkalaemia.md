# Hyperkalaemia in adults: emergency treatment

Source: based on UK Kidney Association, Clinical practice guidelines: treatment of acute hyperkalaemia in adults (2020, updated 2023). Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local hyperkalaemia protocol.

## Severity
Mild hyperkalaemia is a serum potassium of 5.5 to 5.9 mmol/L, moderate is 6.0 to 6.4 mmol/L and severe is 6.5 mmol/L or more. Exclude a falsely raised result (haemolysed sample) but do not delay treatment if the patient is unwell or the ECG is abnormal.

## ECG changes
ECG changes of hyperkalaemia include tall peaked T waves, flattened or absent P waves, a prolonged PR interval, a widened QRS complex, a sine-wave pattern, bradycardia and ventricular tachycardia or fibrillation. A normal ECG does not rule out dangerous hyperkalaemia. Attach a cardiac monitor in moderate or severe hyperkalaemia.

## Protect the heart: IV calcium
If potassium is 6.5 mmol/L or more, or there are ECG changes, give IV calcium: 10 mL of 10% calcium chloride or 30 mL of 10% calcium gluconate, over 5 to 10 minutes. Repeat the ECG; further doses may be needed if ECG changes persist. Calcium does not lower potassium.

## Shift potassium into cells: insulin and glucose
Give 10 units of soluble insulin (such as Actrapid) with 25 g of glucose IV over 15 minutes. Check capillary blood glucose regularly for at least 6 hours because of the risk of hypoglycaemia. If the pre-treatment blood glucose is below 7.0 mmol/L, a 10% glucose infusion is recommended afterwards to prevent hypoglycaemia. Nebulised salbutamol 10 to 20 mg can be added for severe hyperkalaemia.

## Remove potassium and treat the cause
Stop potassium supplements and drugs that raise potassium where possible. Potassium binders such as sodium zirconium cyclosilicate or patiromer remove potassium from the body. Treat the underlying cause, such as acute kidney injury. Seek urgent renal advice if hyperkalaemia does not respond, as dialysis may be needed. Recheck potassium within 1 to 2 hours of treatment.
