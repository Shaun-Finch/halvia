# Hypoglycaemia in adults in hospital

Source: based on the Joint British Diabetes Societies for Inpatient Care (JBDS-IP), The hospital management of hypoglycaemia in adults with diabetes mellitus. Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local hypoglycaemia protocol.

## Definition
Hypoglycaemia is a blood glucose below 4.0 mmol/L. Symptoms include sweating, shaking, palpitations, hunger, confusion, drowsiness, odd behaviour and, when severe, seizures or unconsciousness. Some people have reduced awareness of hypoglycaemia.

## Conscious, orientated and able to swallow
Give 15 to 20 g of quick-acting carbohydrate, for example 150 to 200 mL of pure fruit juice or 4 to 5 glucose tablets. Recheck the capillary blood glucose after 10 to 15 minutes. If it is still below 4.0 mmol/L, repeat, up to three times in total. If still low after three cycles, consider IV 10% glucose or IM glucagon 1 mg and call a doctor.

## Conscious but confused, uncooperative or aggressive
If the patient can swallow, give quick-acting carbohydrate or 1.5 to 2 tubes of glucose gel squeezed into the mouth between the teeth and gums. If this fails, give IM glucagon 1 mg (once only) or IV glucose.

## Unconscious, fitting or very aggressive, or nil by mouth
Check airway, breathing and circulation, and stop any IV insulin. If there is IV access, give 75 to 80 mL of 20% glucose or 150 to 160 mL of 10% glucose over 10 to 15 minutes. If there is no IV access, give glucagon 1 mg IM; it may be less effective in people with prolonged starvation, alcohol excess or severe liver disease. Recheck glucose after 10 minutes and repeat if still below 4.0 mmol/L.

## After the episode
When the glucose is above 4.0 mmol/L and the patient has recovered, give a longer-acting carbohydrate such as two biscuits, a slice of bread or the next meal containing carbohydrate. Do not omit insulin in people with type 1 diabetes, though the dose may need review. Find the cause, review medicines, and document and escalate as needed.
