# Exacerbation of COPD in hospital

Source: based on NICE guideline NG115, Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease in over 16s (updated 2019), and the BTS guideline for oxygen use in adults (2017). Summary written by Halvia. Follow your local COPD pathway.

## Oxygen
Many people with COPD are at risk of hypercapnic respiratory failure. Aim for a target oxygen saturation of 88 to 92%, using controlled oxygen through a Venturi mask (24% or 28%) or nasal cannulae, until an arterial or capillary blood gas is known. Check blood gases on arrival and after changes in oxygen.

## Medicines
Give inhaled or nebulised short-acting bronchodilators, such as salbutamol and ipratropium. In people with hypercapnia or acidosis, drive nebulisers with compressed air rather than oxygen, giving supplemental oxygen by nasal cannulae. Give oral prednisolone 30 mg daily for 5 days unless contraindicated. Give antibiotics if sputum is purulent or there are signs of pneumonia, following local guidance.

## Non-invasive ventilation (NIV)
Consider NIV for people with persistent hypercapnic respiratory failure (pH below 7.35 and PaCO2 above 6.5 kPa) despite optimal medical treatment. Agree a treatment escalation plan, including what to do if NIV fails.

## Other checks
Do a chest X-ray, ECG, full blood count, electrolytes and theophylline level if taking theophylline. Look for pneumonia, pneumothorax, heart failure and pulmonary embolism.
