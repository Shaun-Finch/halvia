# Recognising sepsis in adults

Source: based on NICE guideline NG51, Suspected sepsis: recognition, diagnosis and early management (first published 2016, updated 2024), and the UK Sepsis Trust. Summary written by Halvia. Always check the current NICE guideline and your local sepsis pathway.

## What sepsis is
Sepsis is a life-threatening reaction to an infection, in which the body's response damages its own tissues and organs. It can follow any infection, including chest, urine, abdominal, skin and line infections. Early recognition and treatment save lives.

## Think sepsis
Consider sepsis in anyone with signs or symptoms that suggest an infection who is unwell or deteriorating, even if their temperature is normal. People at higher risk include the very old, people who are frail or immunocompromised, people on chemotherapy, people who have recently had surgery or an invasive procedure, people with indwelling lines or catheters, people who inject drugs, and people who are pregnant or have recently given birth.

## Using NEWS2
For people aged 16 and over in acute hospital settings, the 2024 update to NICE NG51 uses NEWS2 to stratify the risk of severe illness or death from suspected sepsis, alongside clinical judgement. A higher score means a faster review by a senior clinician and faster treatment. Check the guideline for the exact timings for review and antibiotics at each score.

## High-risk features in adults
Features that suggest a high risk of severe illness or death from sepsis include: objective evidence of new altered mental state; a respiratory rate of 25 breaths per minute or more, or a new need for 40% oxygen or more to keep oxygen saturation above 92% (above 88% in known COPD); a heart rate above 130 beats per minute; a systolic blood pressure of 90 mmHg or less, or more than 40 mmHg below normal; not having passed urine in the previous 18 hours, or a urine output below 0.5 ml/kg per hour in a catheterised patient; mottled or ashen appearance; cyanosis of the skin, lips or tongue; and a non-blanching rash of the skin.

## Immediate actions often used (Sepsis Six)
The UK Sepsis Trust's Sepsis Six is a set of actions to deliver within an hour once sepsis is suspected: take blood cultures, take a blood lactate, give intravenous antibiotics, give intravenous fluids if indicated, give oxygen if needed to reach target saturations, and monitor urine output. Escalate to a senior clinician and consider critical care input. Follow your local pathway.

## Safety-netting
For people at lower risk who go home, give clear advice on what to look out for and when to seek urgent help, such as new confusion, breathlessness, mottled skin, not passing urine or feeling much worse.
